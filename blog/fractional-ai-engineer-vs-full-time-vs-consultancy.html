Fractional & Senior AI Engineering

Fractional AI Engineer vs Full-Time Hire vs Consultancy: Which Is Right for Your Agentic AI Project?

GJ
By Gangadevi JeyarajSenior Agentic AI Engineer · CPO, Enthiram.ai · 15 years in AI
23 November 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Choose a fractional AI engineer when you need senior, hands-on leadership part-time and must start fast. Choose a full-time hire when AI is core and needs someone every day. Choose a consultancy when you need scale, breadth or a one-off decision. Many companies start fractional to get the first system right, then hire full-time once the direction is proven.

Key takeaways

  • The three differ most on commitment, cost and who owns the outcome day to day.
  • Fractional gives you senior delivery part-time; full-time gives you daily ownership at full cost; consultancy gives you scale and breadth at the highest day rate.
  • The expensive mistake is a full-time hire before you know what to build, or a consultancy when you needed one focused expert.
  • Starting fractional and hiring later is often the lowest-risk path for a first agentic AI project.

Once a company accepts that it needs senior AI help, the next decision is how to buy it: a fractional engineer, a full-time hire, or a consultancy. I get asked to compare these honestly, including where I am not the right answer. Here is how I think it through.

The three options in one view

Commitment Part-time, ongoing retainer Permanent, full-time Project, fixed term
Starts in Days Months to recruit Weeks

No option is best in general. Each is best for a situation.

When a full-time hire is right

If AI is central to your product and someone needs to own it every single day, hire full-time. A permanent senior engineer builds deep knowledge of your systems, is always available, and grows with the company. That depth is worth the full cost when the load is genuinely full-time and continuous.

The trap is hiring full-time too early. Recruiting a senior AI engineer takes months in every major hub, senior people are scarce, and if you are still unsure what to build, you may hire the wrong specialist for a direction that changes. Paying a full salary while the strategy is still forming is the most expensive way to learn.

When a consultancy or agency is right

Choose a consultancy when you need breadth or scale that one person cannot provide: several workstreams at once, a large delivery against a fixed deadline, or a formal strategy study with a brand name attached for the board. Agencies bring a team and a process, and they can move a lot of work in parallel.

The trade-offs are cost and ownership. Day rates are the highest of the three, output can be generic because the firm reuses patterns across clients, and the people who learned your business leave when the engagement ends. Insist on a real handover, or the knowledge walks out of the door.

When a fractional engineer is right

Choose a fractional senior AI engineer when you need experienced, hands-on leadership but not a full-time cost, and you need to move now. This is the common case for a first agentic AI project: the work is real, the right architecture matters, and a wrong early decision would cost months, but the job is a few focused months or part-time ongoing, not a permanent full load.

A fractional engineer gives you the senior judgment of a full-time hire with the speed and flexibility of a consultant, and unlike a consultancy, it is one accountable person who builds the core themselves and mentors your team. The limit is honest: one part-time person cannot deliver a large multi-team programme on a tight deadline. When the work grows beyond that, the fractional engineer should help you hire or bring in a team, not pretend to be one.

How I would choose, in order

Four questions, in this order, settle most cases:

  1. Is AI core to the product and needed every day? If yes, hire full-time. A fractional engineer can bridge you while you recruit.
  2. Do you already know what to build? If not, start fractional. Paying for senior judgment to get the direction right is far cheaper than a full-time hire or a consultancy building the wrong thing.
  3. Do you need many people working at once? If yes, a consultancy or agency fits the scale. If you need one expert to get one important thing right, fractional is better and cheaper.
  4. Must you start now? A fractional engineer starts in days. A hire takes months. A consultancy takes weeks and a procurement process.

The path most first projects should take

For a company's first serious agentic AI effort, the lowest-risk path is usually to start fractional. Bring in a senior engineer part-time to decide what to build, ship the first real system, put the safety and evaluation in place, and train the in-house developers. Once the direction is proven and the load is clearly full-time, hire a permanent engineer, now from a position of knowledge, with a working system and a trained team to join. The fractional engineer can even help you write the role and interview for it.

That sequence avoids the two expensive mistakes: a premature full-time hire for an unproven direction, and a consultancy engagement when what you needed was one expert who would stay close to the work.

What changes in 2027?

As agentic AI becomes standard, I expect the fractional-first path to become the default for mid-sized companies. The scarcity of senior talent makes full-time-first slow and risky, and the pace of change rewards getting a real system live quickly and learning from it. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a fractional AI engineer and a full-time hire?

A fractional AI engineer works part-time on an ongoing retainer and starts in days, giving you senior hands-on leadership without a full-time cost. A full-time hire is a permanent employee who owns AI every day and builds deep in-house knowledge, at full salary, benefits and a months-long recruitment process.

When should I use an AI consultancy instead of a fractional engineer?

Use a consultancy when you need breadth or scale that one person cannot provide, such as several workstreams at once, a large fixed-deadline delivery, or a formal strategy study. Use a fractional engineer when you need one accountable expert to get one important thing right and to stay close to the work.

Is a fractional AI engineer cheaper than a full-time hire?

Usually, for the right scope. You pay a monthly retainer for part-time work instead of a full salary, benefits, equity and recruitment cost, and the engineer starts immediately rather than after months of hiring. For a few focused months or ongoing part-time work, that is the cheaper route to a working system.

Can I start fractional and hire full-time later?

Yes, and it is often the best path for a first agentic AI project. A fractional engineer sets the direction, ships the first system, puts safety and evaluation in place and trains your team, then helps you recruit a permanent engineer once the direction is proven.

Which is right for a first agentic AI project?

For most first projects, start fractional. It gives you senior judgment to decide what to build and a real system shipped quickly, at low cost and low risk, before you commit to a full-time hire or a large consultancy engagement.
